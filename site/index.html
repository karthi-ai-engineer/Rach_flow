Features

Dictation that stays out of the way

A small app with one job: turn what you say into text, wherever you are typing.

Works in every app

Chat, email, documents, code editors, the browser: the text goes where your cursor is. Your clipboard is left as it was.

Recognised on your laptop

NVIDIA Parakeet turns speech into text on your own computer, so your voice never leaves it. Or Whisper for 99 languages, a cloud model with your key, or your own server.

Hold, or go hands-free

Hold Ctrl+Win while you talk, or tap it to keep recording and tap again to finish. Esc cancels.

Translate with CtrlCC

Select text in any app and press Ctrl+C twice: a small window shows it translated. Copy it with C, or replace the text with ↵.

Transform it afterwards

Say "make it concise", "professional", "bullet points" or "action items", or double-tap Ctrl for a menu. Numbers, names and your "maybe" stay.

Your words and snippets

Names and terms spelled your way, sound-alikes fixed ("post grass" → PostgreSQL), and "my email" typing your email address, exactly as you wrote it.

Clean text, if you want it

An AI model you choose (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini, Groq, or Ollama and vLLM on your own server) adds punctuation and drops the "um"s. Only text is sent, never audio.

Always shows what it's doing

A small pill at the bottom of the screen: your voice while you speak, then "Typed". It never steals the focus.

Updates itself

When a new version is out, Rflow says so. One click downloads it, checks it against its published checksum and installs it.

Live translation

A meeting, a video or the people in the room, translated while they speak, in a bar you can move anywhere. A voice on your laptop can speak it, too.

Know what it costs

Ready-made setups pick the cheapest model that does the job well, about $1 a month for typical use, or nothing at all on your PC. Expensive models are marked in red.

Free and open source

No account, no subscription, no tracking. The code is on GitHub under the MIT License: read it, change it, share it.

The app

One calm window, ten clear sections

Open Rflow from the Start menu or its icon near the clock. Closing the window keeps Rflow running, so dictation still works everywhere.

Over any app

Translate and transform where you write

Select text and press CtrlCC: the translation appears at the pointer, with the language at the top. Double-tap Ctrl for Text Transform and press 1–4. The popups never take the focus, so your app keeps its selection, and every action has its key.

FAQ

Questions

Is my voice sent anywhere?

Not with Parakeet or Whisper: they recognise speech on your laptop. If you choose a cloud model (OpenAI, Google Gemini, Groq), each recording goes to that provider, and Rflow asks you first; with your own server, it goes to that server. The AI cleanup, if you switch it on, sends only the finished text (never audio) to the provider you chose; Text Transform and Translate send the text you select to it, too. Live translation sends what it listens to (the computer's sound, the microphone, or both: other people's voices too) to Google Gemini, and says so before it starts. Your API keys are encrypted for your Windows account.

What does it cost?

Rflow itself is free and open source. Cloud models are paid to the provider, with your own key, and Rflow shows what each one costs a month for typical use (20 minutes of dictation a day): about $1 for the Recommended setup, nothing for Local. Expensive models are marked in red before you choose them.

Is it really open source?

Yes, under the MIT License. The code, the issues and every change are on GitHub; bug reports, ideas and pull requests are welcome.

Windows says "Windows protected your PC"

The installer isn't code-signed yet. Click More info, then Run anyway.

What does it need?

Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit), a microphone and about 1 GB of disk space: about 300 MB for Rflow and 660 MB for Parakeet. No GPU. The internet is needed once, to download Parakeet; after that only for a cloud model, the optional cleanup and updates.

Which languages?

English with Parakeet. Whisper large-v3 turbo (a 1.6 GB download, quick with an NVIDIA graphics card) and the cloud models know many more, Tamil and Japanese among them.

How do updates work?

Rflow checks for a new version when it starts and every few hours, and shows a banner in its window: click Update now. The download is checked against its published checksum before it's installed, and Rflow restarts itself.

Nothing is typed in one particular app

That app probably runs as administrator; Windows doesn't let normal programs type into those.
